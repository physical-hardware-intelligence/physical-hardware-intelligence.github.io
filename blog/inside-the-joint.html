    Inside the Joint

    Part 1 · The basics

    01 · Anatomy

    What is inside a robot joint?

    Almost every electric robot joint is four parts in a row.

    A motor makes torque. A reducer, or gearbox, trades the motor's speed for more torque. A sensor measures the joint's angle. A driver board sets the motor current to reach the angle it was asked for.

    Every design on this page answers one question: how much do you gear down, and with what? We cover the basics, then walk down that ladder, from our SO-101's servo to joints with almost no gears, then look at other ways to move.

    Go deeper terms
    Torque
    Turning force: force times its distance from the axis, in newton-metres (N·m). 1 N·m is about holding a 1 kg bag 10 cm from a pivot (0.98 N·m).
    Reducer
    Gears that make the output turn slower and stronger than the motor.
    Encoder
    The angle sensor. Often a magnet on the shaft, read by a chip.
    Control loop
    Measure, compare with the target, correct, many times a second.
    Check yourself one question

    Which part would you change to make a joint that can tell how hard it is pushing?

    Usually the reducer. The motor's current already tells its torque, but a high-ratio gearbox hides it behind friction. Gear down less, add a spring you can measure, or add a torque sensor.

    02 · The motor

    Why can't a motor just turn the joint?

    A motor is a ring of magnets and a ring of copper coils. Switch the coils on in turn and the magnets are always pulled forward, so the rotor spins; in the model the coils glow as they switch. More current means more torque.

    But motors work best spinning fast with little torque, and joints need the opposite. MIT's Cheetah team found that a motor strong enough to drive their leg with no gears would have been 76.2 cm across.[W] So nearly every robot puts gears after the motor.

    Go deeper terms and maths
    Brushed, brushless
    Brushed motors switch their coils with sliding contacts; brushless motors switch them with a chip.
    FOC
    Field-oriented control: a way of driving a brushless motor that keeps the magnetic pull pointing the most useful way.
    Stall torque
    The most torque a motor makes, at zero speed. It overheats if held there.
    Rated torque
    What it can give all day without overheating.

    Each amp of current adds the same amount of torque:

    \[ \tau = K_t\, I \]

    \(\tau\) torque · \(I\) current · \(K_t\) the motor's torque constant, in N·m per amp.

    The STS3215's sheet lists \(K_t\) = 8 kg·cm/A at its output, and its stall torque divided by its stall current agrees: 0.785 vs 0.765 N·m/A (2.5% apart).[F]

    Check yourself one question

    You double the current through a motor held still. What happens to its torque, and what's the catch?

    The torque doubles. The heat quadruples (it grows with current squared), which is why rated torque is far below stall torque.

    03 · The trade

    What does a gearbox trade?

    A small gear driving one with four times the teeth turns four times slower and pushes four times harder, like a bike's lowest gear. That multiplier is the gear ratio, \(N\). The model shows 4:1.

    The hidden cost: from the output, the motor's spinning rotor feels \(N^2\) times heavier.[W] So a high-ratio joint is strong and cheap but stiff: push it and it barely moves; hit something and the gear teeth take the blow. A low-ratio joint is weaker per motor, but you can push it, and it can feel you push.

    The ladder this page walks down

    Go deeper terms and maths
    Gear ratio, \(N\)
    How many motor turns make one output turn.
    Inertia
    How hard something is to spin up or stop.
    Backdrivable
    You can turn the motor by pushing on the output.
    Efficiency, \(\eta\)
    The share of torque that survives the gears' friction (0.9 = 90%).

    Torque goes up by \(N\), speed goes down by \(N\):

    \[ \tau_{out} = \eta\,N\,\tau_m, \qquad \omega_{out} = \omega_m / N \]

    Why the felt mass grows with \(N^2\): to move the output, the rotor must spin \(N\) times faster, and spinning energy grows with speed squared.

    \[ J_{felt} = N^2 J_m \]

    \(\omega\) speed · \(J\) inertia · \(m\) motor side, \(out\) output side. At 345:1, \(N^2\) = 119,025. (Wensing et al. 2017, eq. 43.)

    Check yourself one question

    Our SO-101 leader uses 1/147 servos at the wrist, the follower 1/345.[L] How much heavier does the follower's wrist feel to push?

    \((345/147)^2 \approx\) 5.5 times. That's why the arm a person moves uses lower-ratio servos.

    Part 2 · Down the gear-ratio ladder, from 345:1 to 6:1

    04 · Our servo · 345:1

    What is inside the servo that moves our SO-101?

    The top of the ladder. The Feetech STS3215 packs a whole joint into 55 g: a small DC motor, a chain of small gears (345:1), a magnet on the output read by a 12-bit sensor, and a board that holds a position.[F]

    1.91 N·mstall torque, 7.4 V
    0.49 N·mrated torque
    52 rpmtop speed, no load
    0.088°one sensor step

    You can only send it a position: it has no current loop, so you can't ask for a force.[Φ] Its rotor feels 119,025 times heavier from the output, one reason the arm is hard to move by hand even with the power off. Its gears allow up to 0.5° of slack, about 0.87 mm at a point 10 cm out. The model's gears are drawn at about 230:1, since Feetech doesn't publish the real tooth counts.

    Go deeper terms and maths
    Backlash
    Slack between gear teeth: the output can wiggle a little without the motor moving.
    12-bit sensor
    Counts \(2^{12}\) = 4096 steps per turn.
    Position loop, P gain
    The servo pushes in proportion to how far it is from the target; the P gain sets how hard.
    Current loop
    A controller that regulates current, and so torque. The STS3215 doesn't have one.

    One sensor step is a full turn split 4096 ways: \(360°/4096 =\) 0.088°. Slack at a distance is distance times angle: \(0.1\,\text{m} \times \tan 0.5° =\) 0.87 mm.

    The code what LeRobot writes into every servo

    lerobot/robots/so_follower/so_follower.py, lines 163–173, LeRobot 0.6.0 (trimmed):

    self.bus.write("Operating_Mode", motor, OperatingMode.POSITION.value)
self.bus.write("P_Coefficient", motor, 16)
self.bus.write("I_Coefficient", motor, 0)
self.bus.write("D_Coefficient", motor, 32)
if motor == "gripper":
    self.bus.write("Max_Torque_Limit", motor, 500)  # 50% of max torque
    self.bus.write("Protection_Current", motor, 250)  # 50% of max current

    A loop with only a P gain needs an error to push, so a loaded joint settles slightly short of its target. We measured 16.43 mm of sag on our arm.[Φ]

    Check yourself one question

    Listings say the STS3215 makes "30 kg·cm". Why doesn't our SO-101 get that?

    That's the 12 V version at stall (2.94 N·m).[D] The SO-101 uses the 7.4 V version: 19.5 kg·cm at stall, 5 kg·cm rated.[F]

    05 · Harmonic drive · 30–160:1

    How do you get 100:1 in one thin stage?

    Our servo needs four gear stages for 345:1, and each adds slack. A harmonic drive gets a high ratio in one stage with almost none. An oval cam spins inside a thin steel cup with teeth on the outside. The cup bends into an oval and meshes with a rigid ring only at the oval's two ends. The ring has two more teeth, so each cam turn the cup slips back two teeth.[H]

    A 200-tooth cup in a 202-tooth ring gives 100:1; the model uses bigger teeth (20:1) so you can see the bend. The catch: the joint can't feel contact through its motor, so arms that need feel add torque sensors, as Franka does in all seven joints.[Fr]

    Go deeper terms and maths
    Wave generator
    The oval cam; the input.
    Flexspline
    The thin flexible cup; the output.
    Circular spline
    The rigid ring; fixed.
    Strain wave
    The other name: the gear works by bending (straining) the cup in a travelling wave.

    Each cam turn, the cup moves back \(Z_c - Z_f\) teeth out of its \(Z_f\). So a full cup turn takes \(Z_f / (Z_c - Z_f)\) cam turns:

    \[ N = \frac{Z_f}{Z_c - Z_f} \]

    \(Z_f\) cup teeth · \(Z_c\) ring teeth. The output turns opposite to the input. Typical ratios run 30:1 to 160:1 (320:1 on the maker's German site).[H]

    Check yourself one question

    A 102-tooth ring and a 100-tooth cup: what ratio?

    50:1, turning opposite to the input.

    06 · Cycloidal drive

    Which reducer can you 3D-print and trust?

    A harmonic drive's thin cup is delicate and hard to make. A cycloidal drive uses a chunky disc instead. An off-centre cam makes a lobed disc wobble inside a ring of pins. The disc has one lobe fewer than there are pins, so each wobble walks it back one lobe, and pins through holes in the disc carry that slow turn to the output. The model has 11 lobes and 12 pins: 11:1.

    Many lobes touch pins at once, sharing the load. That's why Berkeley Humanoid Lite prints its gearboxes this way: shared load suits plastic, and rounded lobes forgive a desktop printer's limited detail. Its 22-actuator robot costs $4,312 in US parts.[B]

    Go deeper terms and maths
    Eccentric cam
    A disc mounted off-centre on the input shaft, so it makes the cycloidal disc wobble.
    Lobe
    One rounded bump on the disc's edge.
    Two discs
    Real drives usually stack two discs half a turn apart, so their wobbles cancel.

    Each wobble moves the disc back \(P - L\) lobes. A full turn of the disc is \(L\) lobes, so it takes \(L/(P-L)\) wobbles:

    \[ N = \frac{L}{P - L} \]

    \(L\) lobes · \(P\) pins.

    Check yourself one question

    20 pins and 19 lobes: what ratio?

    19:1.

    07 · The YAM's motors · 40:1 and 10:1

    What moves the YAM arms we're getting next?

    Our lab's next setup, the i2RT ABC Research Kit, is two YAM arms.[KIT] Every joint is a Damiao motor: the four parts from section 01 sealed in a 57 mm can, with its own driver board and two encoders.[OA]

    DM4340 ×3base, shoulder, elbow · 40:1 · 27 N·m peak · 362 g
    DM4310 ×3wrist · 10:1 · 7 N·m peak · 300 g
    DM4310 ×1standard gripper

    The DM4340 matches our servo's top speed (52 rpm) with 14× its torque, and its rotor feels 1,600× heavier instead of 119,025×. The 10:1 DM4310 is in quasi-direct-drive territory; OpenArm's docs say the 40:1 DM4340 is not.[OA]

    The real change is control. Instead of a position, the software sends each motor a target, two gains and an extra torque every 4 ms, and the motor turns that into current. It also cancels gravity and friction, so the arms start out floating: you teach a YAM by moving it by hand. If commands stop for 400 ms, the motors switch to damping and the arm sinks gently.[I]

    Standard and Pro tiers list the same motors in the software; Ultra 2 adds a DM4340 at joint 4; BIG YAM uses DM6248 shoulders.[ID] A standard two-arm kit has 6 DM4340s and 8 DM4310s.

    Go deeper terms and maths
    CAN bus
    The shared wire the motors and the computer talk over.
    Gain
    How strongly a controller reacts.
    Gravity compensation
    Adding exactly the torque that cancels the arm's weight.
    Coulomb friction
    The roughly constant drag a joint has whenever it moves.
    Damping mode
    The motor resists motion in proportion to speed, like moving through honey.

    Each motor acts like a virtual spring plus a shock absorber, plus an extra push:

    \[ \tau = k_p\,(q_{target} - q) + k_d\,(\dot q_{target} - \dot q) + \tau_{ff} \]

    \(q\) angle · \(\dot q\) speed · \(k_p\) the spring, pulling toward the target · \(k_d\) the shock absorber, resisting speed · \(\tau_{ff}\) the push the software adds for gravity and friction. YAM defaults: \(k_p\) 80, \(k_d\) 5 on the 40:1 joints; 10 and 1.5 at the wrist. Set \(k_p = k_d = 0\) and only \(\tau_{ff}\) is left: the arm floats.

    Check yourself one question

    Powered on, you can push a YAM arm by hand but not our SO-101 follower. Why?

    The YAM's motors run torque control with gravity compensation at 40:1 and 10:1, so they can yield. The STS3215 holds a stiff position behind 345:1, with no torque mode.

    08 · Quasi-direct drive · 6–10:1

    What if the motor could feel what the joint touches?

    Lower still is quasi-direct drive (QDD): a wide, flat motor with one planetary stage of about 6–10:1. The model shows 6:1: a sun gear in the middle, three planets, a fixed ring.

    With so little gearing, force flows both ways: push the output and the motor turns, so motor current measures force. MIT's Cheetah (5.8:1) controlled its foot forces this way with no springs or force sensors, and absorbed landings by letting its legs give.[W]

    17 N·mMini Cheetah joint peak; 40 rad/s top[K]
    22 N·mCubeMars AK80-9 peak, 9:1, 490 g[C]
    250 WMini Cheetah actuator peak power[K]

    A surprise: per kilogram, our servo's stall torque (35 N·m/kg) is close to a QDD module's (45). What it lacks is speed and feel: roughly 2.6 W of peak power, about 96× less than a Mini Cheetah joint. QDD's costs are bigger motors, current spent just holding loads, and a higher price.

    Go deeper terms and maths
    Planetary gear
    A central sun gear drives planet gears that roll inside a ring; the frame holding the planets is the output.
    Quasi-direct
    "Almost direct": so little gearing that the motor still feels the load.
    Proprioceptive
    Sensing force through the motor itself, with no separate force sensor.
    Peak power
    The most work per second the actuator can do.

    Current reads as joint torque only if little is lost in the gears; friction is multiplied by \(N\) too, so at high ratios it drowns the signal:

    \[ \tau_{joint} \approx \eta\,N\,K_t\,I \]

    Peak power, assuming torque falls in a straight line from stall to top speed: power peaks at half of each, and half times half is a quarter.

    \[ P_{max} \approx \tfrac14\,\tau_{stall}\,\omega_0 \]

    For our servo: 1.91 × 5.45 / 4 ≈ 2.6 W (an estimate, not a measurement).

    Check yourself one question

    Why does a QDD leg survive landing from a jump better than one with a 100:1 gearbox?

    At 100:1 the rotor feels 10,000× heavier, so the leg can't give and the gear teeth take the impact. At 6:1 it feels 36× heavier, so the impact turns the motor back and the controller absorbs it.

    Part 3 · Other ways to move a joint

    09 · Screws

    How can a small motor lift a piano?

    Sometimes a robot needs a straight push, like a muscle. A screw makes one: each turn moves the nut one lead, a few millimetres. A motor making 1 N·m on a 4 mm lead pushes with about 1,414 N.

    Ball screws roll balls between screw and nut. Planetary roller screws, in the model, use threaded rollers, which carry more load and shock in the same size.[R] At AI Day 2022, Tesla showed an Optimus actuator lifting a half-ton piano.[T] Outsiders infer a roller screw inside; Tesla hasn't said.[Fi]

    Go deeper terms and maths
    Lead
    How far the nut travels per screw turn.
    Ball screw
    Steel balls roll in the thread, so there is little friction.
    Roller screw
    Threaded rollers replace the balls, with more contact for more load.

    Work in equals work out. One turn puts in \(2\pi\) times the torque and moves the nut one lead:

    \[ F\,\ell = 2\pi\,\eta\,\tau \;\Rightarrow\; F = \frac{2\pi\,\eta\,\tau}{\ell} \]

    \(F\) push · \(\ell\) lead · \(\eta\) efficiency. With 1 N·m, 4 mm and 90%: 1,414 N (example numbers).

    Check yourself one question

    You halve the lead. What happens to force and speed?

    Force doubles and speed halves, exactly like doubling a gear ratio.

    10 · Series elastic

    Why put a spring in a joint on purpose?

    Another way to make a stiff joint feel: put a spring between gearbox and load. How much it squeezes tells you the force, and it takes shocks before the gear teeth do.

    Pratt and Williamson introduced the idea in 1995 for MIT's Cog robot.[P] Rethink's Baxter had one in every joint;[Ba] its successor Sawyer moved to titanium springs.[S] ANYbotics' ANYdrive gives 40 N·m peak in 0.9 kg.[A] The cost: a springy joint positions more slowly and less precisely.

    Go deeper terms and maths
    Stiffness, \(k\)
    How much torque it takes to twist the spring by a given angle.
    Deflection
    How far the spring is twisted or squeezed.

    A spring is a scale: torque equals stiffness times twist.

    \[ \tau = k\,\Delta\theta \]

    Stiffer springs measure and position better, but take shocks worse.

    Check yourself one question

    A 300 N·m/rad spring twisted 0.05 rad: what torque?

    15 N·m (example numbers).

    11 · The rest of the family

    What else moves robots?

    Boston Dynamics' product Atlas, shown in January 2026, is all-electric, with 56 degrees of freedom, and lifts 50 kg.[BD]

    Go deeper terms
    Degrees of freedom
    The number of independent ways a robot can move, roughly its number of joints.
    Tendon
    A cable that transmits pull from a distant motor, like the tendons in your forearm.

    13 · Back to our arms

    What does this mean for our arms?

    • The SO-101 is a position arm. Fine for cheap imitation learning; not for force control.
    • Its leader is easy to move because of \(N^2\): the 1/147 wrist feels about 5.5× lighter than the follower's 1/345.
    • The YAM can be taught by hand and made soft on contact, and its motor currents give a rough sense of force. None of that is possible on the STS3215.
    • The YAM isn't a QDD arm. At the 40:1 shoulder the SDK compensates 0.3 N·m of friction, five times the wrist's 0.06, so force estimates should be cleaner at the wrist.

    Corrections to common claims

    • "30 kg·cm" is the 12 V STS3215 at stall. The SO-101's 7.4 V version stalls at 19.5 kg·cm and is rated 5 kg·cm.
    • Sources disagree on whether the STS3215's motor is iron-core or coreless, and none says brushed.
    • Tesla hasn't published Optimus's actuator internals; "roller screw" is an outside inference.
    • Not every Damiao joint motor is QDD: OpenArm says the 40:1 DM4340 isn't.
    • The torque limits in the i2rt driver (28 and 10 N·m) are the CAN command's scaling range, not ratings; the datasheet peaks are 27 and 7.
